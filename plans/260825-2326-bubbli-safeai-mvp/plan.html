The problem

Children use AI chat tools with either no parental oversight or total surveillance. Neither respects the child. Bubbli gates on severity: a conversation opens to a parent only when the safety layer flags it at medium or above.

The hard part

The guardrail engine needs plaintext to work, and the model provider receives plaintext regardless. So parent exclusion is an access‑control and audit guarantee, not a cryptographic one. The plan says so plainly rather than implying otherwise.

The prior art

A near‑identical system was reviewed first. Six production‑breaking defects, all in untested code, including a rate limiter that never blocked a single request. Five are designed out by name in the phases below.

Detection without a response path is not a safety feature. The reviewed system found suicidal ideation correctly, then queued it by recency.

Adversarial review

Four hostile lenses, 40 raw findings, deduped to 15 adjudicated plus 12 second‑tier. All accepted. One reviewer claim was rejected on verification.

Ten criticals
  • A blocking legal question gated Phase 1's irreversible audit‑grant decision
  • G1 tested unflagged conversations; the leak surface is the sub‑medium flagged row
  • assertCanAccess was called in Phase 4 and defined nowhere
  • The corpus gate was self‑certifying: same author, same commit, rules and cases
  • Four accepted‑contract items were silently dropped from the safety layer
  • Provider DPA, region, and model access were verified by no step
  • Encryption at rest appeared in zero of seven phases
  • Child profile creation had a success checkbox and no implementation
  • No transactions anywhere, across six writes per turn
  • Unguarded writes sat ahead of the child's crisis response
What the review actually caught

Three of the ten were compression errors, not oversights. The privacy decision reads “access control and audit, and AES‑256 at rest”; the plan wrote “not cryptography” and lost the second half. The second gate layer was dropped on a rationale the PRD refutes on line 267. Five prior‑art defects were designed out while its structural ones came back.

Every absence claim was re‑checked by grep before acceptance. All held. One claim did not: emotional‑safety rules are authorised by the PRD, so that finding was rejected.

Open questions

One blocks. It is a legal question no design choice can answer.

Blocking · Q‑B · gates Phase 3 steps 9–10

Is parent email verification sufficient verifiable parental consent for under‑13?

Splitting the age band at 13 sharpened this rather than removing it. The under‑13 path now needs a defined consent mechanism, and the 13+ path needs confirmation that a lighter one is permissible. Phase 1 has no dependency on it, and Phase 3's other eight steps proceed meanwhile.
