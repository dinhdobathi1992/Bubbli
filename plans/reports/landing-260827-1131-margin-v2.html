Diagnosis

The whole palette lived in 31° of hue.

Ground, text, borders and the accent all sat between 13° and 44° — orange-brown, top to bottom. Nothing separated by hue, only by lightness, which is precisely what reads as muddy sepia. The accent couldn't sing because it was the same colour as the room.

Why Forest. It solves the muddiness with 137° of separation, and it's the only option that says something. Green reads as safe, growing, calm — and no AI product looks like it, so a parent won't mistake Bubbli for another chatbot. Slate and Midnight separate more, but both read as instrument: right for the parent dashboard, cold for a six-year-old. Paper is the strongest if you'd rather lead light — a worried parent may respond better to an open, unguarded page than to any dark one.

Your second point

Two doors, one object.

You asked for “For children” and “For parents” buttons rather than a faint sign-in link. Shown above in the rail as a segmented pair — deliberately one object rather than two competing buttons, so it reads as “which door am I?” navigation and doesn't fight the single primary CTA in the hero.

  • 1Parents side is filled, children's is outlined — the page's job is conversion, and the parent is the decider.
  • 2A child tapping “For children” lands on sign-in, where a paired device offers “Continue” and nothing needs typing.
  • 3Still one primary CTA in the hero. The doors are navigation; “Start free” is the conversion.
  • 4Both are ≥44px tall and keyboard-reachable; on mobile the pair sits full-width under the wordmark.
